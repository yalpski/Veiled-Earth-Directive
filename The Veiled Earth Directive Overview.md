**The world you know is a lie, or at least, incomplete.**

Modern Earth appears ordinary on the surface. Cities hum with technology, nations war with each other while exploiting their own citizens, and humanity goes about its business blissfully unaware of the truth lurkingw just beyond perception. Magic is real. It has always been real. Throughout history, it has shaped empires, toppled kingdoms, and birthed legends that survive today only as myths and fairy tales.

As societies advanced, it became clear that magic’s power is fundamentally incompatible with widespread knowledge. The casual collateral damage surrounding conflicts between near god-like figures like Achilles and Memnon could devastate entire armies of ordinary soldiers deployed around them. Entire nations could be obliterated by a single being’s temper tantrum, and the weak were routinely oppressed by the strong across the world.

So centuries ago, while magic wielders threatened to tear civilization apart, secret societies emerged from the shadows. These orders made a choice: magic would be hoarded, controlled, and hidden from the masses. It would be granted to individuals sparingly and only with purpose. The magical races were all driven into hiding, forced to exist on the outskirts of the world, becoming nothing more than myth and legend to the masses of non-magical humans.

The unforeseen side effect of hoarding magic like this was that the ambient magic around the world began to grow more and more dense over the centuries. In the last few years it has reached such a level that it began condensing into physical forms at random around the globe. This condensation could appear in the form of magical essences, monsters, or vortices.

Magical monsters are only stable for a short period of time in physical form before disintegrating back into ambient magic. The closer they come to disintegration, the less they are able to reason, and the more “berserk” they become. Magical vortices pull in ambient magic from the surrounding area, concentrating it, and causing it to constantly spawn monsters until the flow of magic to the vortex is disrupted and it collapses.

After thousands of years without it, if the existence of magic were to be suddenly revealed to the public, panic would spread like wildfire. Governments would crumble and society itself would fracture as people grappled with the implications. And if history is any guide, those that would surely suffer the most are the innocent.

---

You are an agent of one such secret society. You have been chosen to undergo Soulforging, an ancient ritual that binds magical essences directly to your soul. Magic will infuse your body, mind, and soul, permanently changing you into a being with the potential to rival the gods themselves. But that, of course, comes later. For now, Soulforging will raise you from a Magnitude 0 person (Mag-0s, or “Magoos”, are individuals with little to no innate magic) into a Magnitude 1 being with a full suite of magical abilities and basic physical and mental capabilities on par with olympic athletes and chess grand masters.

You will be placed on a new, prototype team of other Mag-1s from other secret societies around the world, with a singular mission: work together (despite centuries of infighting and rivalry between societies) to hunt down dangerous magical manifestations before they spiral out of control. Protect the innocent. And preserve the secret of magic at all costs.

## Race

- Humans have a totally free choice of any 3 essences  
- Other races have at least 1 mandatory essence based on their race (Snake for a Naga, Blood for a Vampire, Wolf for a Werewolf, Wing for a Harpy, etc.)  
- Each race gains a racial bonus (Player and GM work together to determine what this is)  
- Generally if you can find a myth or legend of a humanoid creature existing on earth (ie. it isn’t a monstrous creature like a hydra), it can probably work as a playable race. See: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythic\_humanoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythic_humanoids)

## Archetype

Determine a general archetype for your character. This will guide what sort of abilities your character picks up. Below are common archetypes, but by no means are players limited to these, they simply serve as starting points for the conversation:

- **Tanks** will want to focus on a few strong hits with most of their resources being spent on mitigating damage for themselves and their team  
- **Ambushers** will want to focus on stealth, perception, a few very heavy hitting abilities, and some escape  
- **Ranged Attackers** will want to focus on having different types of ranged abilities to deal with every situation, sacrificing survivability for damage  
- **Melee Attackers** will want a balance of small to moderate attacks with a reasonable focus on survivability and movement  
- **Healers** will focus on survivability for themselves and resource restoration for their allies  
- **Support** will focus on battlefield control, buffs, debuffs, and flexibility


## Essence Choices

Magic occasionally and spontaneously coalesces into physical Essences in the world. These can be thought of as blocks of variously flavored magic bouillon. It isn’t known how many different essences are possible, and new ones seem to form as society changes. For example, while the “Magic” essence has been forming for as long as records have been kept, the “Gun” essence has been around for a few hundred years, and the “Spork” essence was first recorded in the 1960s.

Essences are categorized by their rarity, from Common to Legendary. These **are not** rankings of power, usefulness, or effectiveness. These are simply categories based on how frequently each individual Essence has been seen in the world.

When an individual is chosen for Soulforging, they pick essences that best suit them (assuming those are available to the Soulforger) or the archetype they are looking to fill.

**Examples:**

A dedicated healer might choose to pick up the Life, Renewal, and Pure essences.

A character interested in tanking might choose Shield, Iron, and Earth.

### Confluence Essences

Certain Essences do not coalesce naturally, and are instead formed by the combination of three Essences being Soulforged together. These are not random, and the same combination of 3 essences will always produce the same Confluence Essence. Recently there has been a push to record what Confluence Essence is formed by each combination of Essences, but it is far from comprehensive. 

For example, researchers know that Soulforging the Adept, Might, and Magic essences onto a person’s soul produces the Force Confluence Essence, but no one knows what mixing the Fork, Spoon, and Hair essences produces.

Known essences and combinations can be found here: [https://veles-verse.com/tools/essence.html](https://veles-verse.com/tools/essence.html) 

### Essence Effects

The mechanical effects of essences aren’t well defined. They serve primarily as a roleplaying guide for flavoring your abilities and guiding your character growth. Each Essence “binds” to an attribute (Power, Finesse, Soul, Wit) and adds its flavor to the abilities tied to those attributes. The decision of which Essence binds to which Attribute is up to the player and the GM. More than anything, this is just to encourage creativity and create interesting roleplaying opportunities.

## Abilities / Skills / Spells

Each character starts with some “generic” abilities that they must assign to attributes. These are: 

- Basic Attack (Very Low Cost, Low Effect)  
- Basic Reaction (Very Low Cost, Low Effect)  
- Basic Perception (No Cost, Low Effect)  
- Basic Persuasion (No Cost, Low Effect)  
- Basic Movement (Very Low Cost, Low Effect).

Each of these skills will be “flavored” based on the related attribute and essence. Note: The terms “ability”, “skill”, and “spell” are entirely interchangeable. There is no mechanical difference between a social skill and a magical ability. Once the five generic abilities have been assigned, the player and GM must work together to fill out the remaining 15 abilities. As abilities increase in Magnitude, they may alter their functionality, gain additional functionality, or change their cost/effect tags.

It is important to note that just because an ability is tied to an attribute doesn’t mean it necessarily uses that attribute’s resource pool. For example, a ranged magic user may have a very heavy hitting spell that costs mana but is tied to their Power attribute. Similarly, the attribute impacts the “flavor” of the ability more so than its function. For example, an increased movement speed ability may present as:

- Power: Super speed through sheer acceleration around obstacles  
- Finesse: Using obstacles to your advantage through parkour-style precision  
- Soul: The ability to phase through obstacles rather than going around them  
- Wit: The ability to judge angles, cut corners, and find shortcuts

Each of these would allow the character to move more quickly across terrain. It is up to the player to figure out which best suits their character, and how it impacts the rest of their abilities.

# The Magnitude Scale

Magical creatures are classified based on the Magnitude Scale, which is used to quickly describe their general level of power. The scale starts at Magnitude 0, which is used to describe any creatures that have little to no magical abilities. Mag-0’s (often derogatively called “Magoos”) make up the vast majority of the world’s population. Once a creature gains a Confluence Essence they advance to Magnitude 1, meaning they now possess a full suite of magical powers. As their power grows, they can advance to Magnitudes 2 and 3, and incredibly rarely 4 and 5\. 

Here is a general guide for how to imagine the capabilities of the different Magnitudes:

| Magnitude | Dice | Description |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Mag-0 | 1d2 | The average human. Pick Joe Schmoe off the street and this is what you get. |
| Mag-0 | 1d6 | The peak of what the average human is likely to accomplish. This person would be considered quite accomplished in their field. |
| Mag-0 | 1d12 | The peak of un-enhanced human capability. These are the gold medal Olympic athletes, chess grandmasters, and exceptional political or strategic minds. |
| Mag-1 | 1d4-1d12 | A Magnitude 1 individual is largely equivalent to a Magnitude 0, except that while a Mag-0 could be expected to *maybe* excel in a single area, the Mag-1 can (and will) reach world-class capabilities in everything. And, of course, can wield magic. |
| Mag-2 | 2d6-2d12 | These individuals have moved beyond human limitations. They can outperform the greatest humans ever recorded with ease and consistency. They are the inspiration for comic book super heroes. They can toss cars, run a full marathon in under an hour, and calculate the air speed velocity of an unladen swallow in their head. |
| Mag-3 | 3d8-3d12 | These are mythological beings of lore. The Heracles, Sigurd, Bhima, and Gilgamesh figures of legend. Individuals who are beyond human capabilities in all ways, and beyond human understanding in most. An individual of this Magnitude is closer to a god than a human. |
| Mag-4 | 4d10-4d12 | Beings on the verge of divinity. Mortal limits no longer meaningfully apply to them. Their actions can reshape regions, topple armies, and leave lasting marks on history. Against mortals they are absolute. Against gods they are credible threats. |
| Mag-5 | 5d12 | While not actual gods, the powers these individuals routinely wield are the equal of any god. An individual achieving this magnitude is beyond rare. It isn't a "once in a generation" type accomplishment, it's a "once in a civilization" one. |

## Character Advancement

Each Magnitude level has a base die associated with it, as well as a number of those dice that must be rolled each time. The game is not designed for Players to be Magoos, so we will disregard Magnitude 0’s for now. That breakdown is as follows:

- Magnitude 1: 1d4  
- Magnitude 2: 2d6  
- Magnitude 3: 3d8  
- Magnitude 4: 4d10  
- Magnitude 5: 5d12

As you may have noticed, the number of dice rolled is equal to the Magnitude level and the faces on the dice also increase by rank. However, a Magnitude 1 character is not always rolling 1d4. In fact, they will likely advance past that very quickly. The way that happens is through explosions during rolls.

# Skills and Dice

Every skill has a die or dice that you roll when you use it. The size and number of dice show how practiced and powerful that skill is.

An untrained Mag-1 skill starts at 1d4. When you roll the highest number on that die (a 4 on the d4), the skill **permanently improves to 1d6**. If you later roll a 6 on that skill, it improves to 1d8, and so on.

The Mag-1 dice chain looks like this:

1d4 → 1d6 → 1d8 → 1d10 → 1d12 → 2d6

Once the character advances to Mag-2:

2d6 → 2d8 → 2d10 → 2d12 → 3d8

## Exploding Dice

When you roll the maximum on a die, that die explodes. This means you immediately roll the die again and add it to the result (or you roll the next die in the chain if this causes the skill to advance), and that new die can also explode.

**Example (see image):**

You roll 1d4 and get a 4\.

The skill advances and you immediately roll 1d6.

You roll a 6 on the d6. It explodes again and the skill advances to 1d8.

You roll 1d8 and get a 5\. The chain stops.

In this single action, your skill jumped from 1d4 all the way to 1d8. Multiple explosions are how you get those big, memorable leaps in power.

| Note: These values are automatically calculated by the roller, you do not need to do this manually. |
| :---- |

**Skill advancement occurs when you roll the maximum die value on a number of dice equal to your Magnitude level.** This sounds confusing, but is simple in practice:

If you are a Mag-2 rolling 2d6 and get a 3 and a 6, the 6 explodes while the 3 does not. When you roll the explosion you do not roll a d8, because you have only rolled a single 6 so far. If you get a 6 on the explosion roll, that means you have now rolled a total of two 6's during this skill test (the requisite number to advance a Mag-2 skill), which means it advances to the d8 level for the remainder of the game (including any further rolls needed due to explosions on this specific check). In order to advance the skill past d8, you need to roll a total of two 8’s on a skill check, and so on.

Once a skill reaches the d12 level, the next advancement pushes it into the first rank of the next Magnitude. For example, a Mag-1 who rolls a 12 on their d12 advances that skill to the base roll of a Mag-2: 2d6. At that point the skill cannot advance further until the character themself advances to Magnitude 2\.

This means advancement through the Magnitude 1 ranks will generally be fast and easy, but the higher up your character advances, the more difficult further advancement becomes.

# Magnitude Advancement

A character cannot advance to the next Magnitude level until all of their attributes have advanced to that rank. Each attribute, in turn, can only advance once enough of their associated skills have reached that rank. 

Recall that each character has 20 total skills divided equally across their 4 attributes, for a total of 5 skills per attribute. Each attribute allows a total exemption of skills equal to the character’s current Magnitude level. That is to say that all skills under an attribute (except for exempt ones) must achieve the next rank before the attribute itself ranks up.

| Note: These values are automatically calculated by the roller, you do not need to do this manually. |
| :---- |

Again, this is easier experienced than explained:

A Magnitude 1 character must advance their Power, Finesse, Soul, and Wit to Magnitude 2 in order for the character as a whole to reach that rank. To advance each, all skills except exempt ones must reach Mag-2 individually. Because the character is Magnitude 1, each of their attributes allows 1 associated skill to be exempt from this requirement. That means their Power can advance to Magnitude 2 when ⅘ of their Power skills reach Mag-2.

Similarly, a Mag-2 character’s Power can advance to Mag-3 when just ⅗ of the associated skills have advanced (they are Mag-2 meaning they can “skip” 2 of the 5 skills getting to Mag-3).

| Attribute Rank Up | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| Mag-1 → Mag-2 | **4 out of 5** skills at Mag-2. |
| Mag-2 → Mag-3 | **3 out of 5** skills at Mag-3. |
| Mag-3 → Mag-4 | **2 out of 5** skills at Mag-4. |
| Mag-4 → Mag-5 | **1 out of 5** skills at Mag-5. |

The player does not need to choose which skills are exempt ahead of time. Their attribute automatically advances the moment the final required skill advances (automatically making the remaining ones the exempt ones).

Early on, you need most skills to advance, so you build a broad foundation. Later, you need fewer skills at higher levels, so you develop sharp specializations. In the end, each attribute might have one truly legendary signature skill.

Every roll is an opportunity to advance, so make as many rolls as you can justify, and focus on the skills you want to improve.

# Character Attributes

Each character has four core attributes: Power, Finesse, Soul, and Wit. Each attribute is assigned a die value, just like skills, but unlike skills they do not advance on their own. Instead, they are equal to the value of the highest skill associated with them. 

For example, a fresh Mag-1 character would have all Power skills at the base 1d4, meaning their power is 1d4 as well. When one of those skills advances to 1d6, so does the character’s power. The only exception to this is that the attribute “sticks” at the d12 level until the requisite number its skills have advanced to the next Magnitude (as described above).

They are rarely rolled on their own, as characters should be trying to use skills instead of attributes whenever possible.

# Character Resources

Your character has four resource pools that represent their life force, physical endurance, magical energy, and ability to recover from strain. These pools are not static numbers. They grow as your skills improve, because each one is based directly on the dice used by the skills within its attribute. When you reach a new Magnitude Level, your resource pools also expand dramatically.

# Combat

## Initiative

The basic flow of combat is designed to be as flexible as the rest of the system. At the start of each round of combat, each participant will choose whether they want to take a Fast Turn or a Slow Turn. For players:

- A **Fast Turn** means you will act before any of the enemies and may take **2 actions** on your turn.  
- A **Slow Turn** means you will act after the Fast enemies but before the Slow enemies, and may take **3 actions** on your turn.

Once each participant has decided what type of turn they would like to take, the round will begin with the players taking Fast Turns. Players sharing a turn may decide which order their characters act in, and may break up their actions however they wish during that turn. If a player wants to take one of their actions first, then save their second action until after a few other players have taken actions, that’s perfectly fine\!

Turn order: Fast Players → Fast Enemies → Slow Players → Slow Enemies

After the Slow Enemies act, everyone picks how they would like to act in the next round.

### Actions

An action during combat is any individual act you are taking. Usually this is using one of your abilities, but it also includes significant movement (turning or taking a few steps isn’t an action, but running across the battlefield is), interacting with an object, or anything else that takes more than a second or two.

### Reactions

Certain abilities are marked as Reactive. Such abilities can be used at any point during combat when their triggering condition is met. Most often this is an ability like *Dodge* or *Block*, which can mitigate the damage from an enemy attack. Other Reactive abilities include traps, wards, and counterattacks.

Reactions can be stacked, but are limited to one use per trigger. For example, a tank archetype character often has several ways to reduce incoming damage. They can attempt to dodge an attack, throw up a magical shield to absorb what they couldn’t dodge, and then block with a physical shield what made it through the magical one. They may do this for every attack made against them, for as long as their Stamina and Mana holds out.

What they can’t do is try to dodge the same attack twice, because there is only a single triggering event (the attack). They can, of course, try to dodge the next attack from that same enemy, because that is a new triggering event.

## Conditions

### Surprise

Often characters can be surprised by combat suddenly breaking out, gaining the Surprised condition:

**Surprised**: Cannot take Fast Turns or use Reactive Abilities. Requires an Action to remove before any other Actions can be taken.

In effect this means any surprised characters must take a Slow Turn in the first round of combat, get only 2 actions on that turn, and cannot use Reactive abilities until after their turn.

### Other Conditions

**Stunned:** Lose 1 Actions per turn and cannot use Reactive abilities.

**Prone:** Cannot use movement abilities or dodge-type Reactive abilities. Can use an Action to stand and remove this condition.

**Grappled:** Cannot use movement abilities or dodge-type Reactive abilities. Other abilities are restricted based on grapple type (determined by the GM). Can use Actions to attempt to break the grapple (Contest Power or Finesse \[or appropriate escape skill\] against the Grappler’s Power \[or Grapple skill\] for each Action spent).

**Bleeding:** You suffer your Magnitude Level in Health damage every time you take an action until this condition is removed. Additional instances of Bleeding are additive. Each stack of Bleeding prevents an amount of healing up to the creature’s Magnitude Level, and is removed once that threshold is surpassed. While Bleeding, transferring Recovery points to Health costs an Action and triggers the Bleeding damage (recovery transferred in this way can remove stacks of Bleeding just like other healing effects).

**Poisoned:** You suffer your Magnitude Level in Health and Stamina damage every round until this condition is removed. Additional instances of Poisoned are additive. Poison can only be cleansed with an appropriate healing or cleansing ability.

**Cursed:** You suffer your Magnitude Level in Health and Mana damage every round until this condition is removed. Additional instances of Cursed are additive. Curses can only be cleansed with an appropriate healing or cleansing ability.

# Death

When a Magoo (Mag-0) is reduced to 0 Health they are killed and their soul is immediately pulled into the Realm of the Dead. No known magic can return the person to life, though animation magic can be used on the corpse.

When a Mag-1 (and above) is reduced to 0 Health they are incapacitated. At the end of the turn, all available Recovery is immediately converted to Health, bringing the individual back to consciousness. If no Recovery is available they are killed, but their soul clings stubbornly to this realm. For up to one minute they can be healed by other means to return them to life. After that time, they can still be resurrected for a number of hours up to their Magnitude level by a resurrection ability of equal or greater Magnitude. After that time, their soul is pulled into the Realm of the Dead and resurrection becomes impossible. Animation magic of equal or greater Magnitude can be used on the corpse.

When a magical monster is reduced to 0 Health it is killed and after about 1 minute will “evaporate” back into ambient magic. Certain resurrection or animation magic used before then can return the monster to “life”.

## Soul Scars

Each time a person is killed and brought back it leaves a permanent scar on their soul. The effects of this scar should be cooperatively determined by the player and GM. These Soul Scars are plainly visible to anyone with magical sight, and often manifest as physical scars as well.
